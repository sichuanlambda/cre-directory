Starter Software Stack for a Small Residential and Multifamily Landlord

At this size the software has three jobs: collect rent and sign leases without chasing anyone, keep books your accountant can use, and price units correctly. All three can start under $20 a month. A full property management system earns its cost when you hire help, pass about 50 to 75 units, or start managing for other owners.

Who this is for

An owner who self-manages single-family homes and small multifamily buildings, roughly 10 to 100 units, with little or no staff. Totals assume 40 units.

Jobs the stack must cover

Leases, rent and listings

Listing syndication, applications, screening, e-signed leases and online rent.

Bookkeeping

Income and expenses by property and tax-ready reports.

Rent pricing

Comps to set and defend rents at renewal and turnover.

Inspections

Move-in and move-out records with photos for deposit disputes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest software setup for a landlord with about 40 units?

$12 a month at published prices: RentRedi's annual plan ($12/mo, unlimited units) for rent, leases and listings, Stessa Essentials for free bookkeeping, and RentCast's free plan for rent comps. TurboTenant's Free plan can bring it to $0 if you do not need online lease signing.

When should a small landlord move to Buildium or a similar system?

When you hire help, pass about 50 to 75 units, or start managing other owners' properties and need owner statements and trust-style accounting. Buildium Essential starts at $62/mo; with Pro rent comps and per-door inspections the fuller stack is from $114 a month at 40 units.

Is AppFolio a good fit for a self-managing landlord?

Rarely at this size. AppFolio is built for management companies, starts at $1.49 per unit with a 50-unit minimum and a minimum spend, and its strengths (owner portals, leasing CRM, trust accounting) matter most when you manage for others.
