Starter Software Stack for an Emerging Sponsor or Syndicator

Your first raise is mostly relationships, a sound model and clean paperwork. Software matters less than it looks in the first deal and a lot more by the second, when investor reporting, distributions and K-1s start to repeat. Start with free underwriting and bookkeeping, and buy an investor portal when the investor count makes email and spreadsheets risky.

Who this is for

A sponsor, or two partners, raising a first fund or the first one or two syndications for multifamily or small commercial assets, with one or two legal entities and no investor relations staff yet.

Jobs the stack must cover

Underwriting

A model investors and lenders can follow: rent roll, operating budget, debt and waterfall.

Market rents

Rent comps to defend the pro forma.

Property bookkeeping

Income and expenses by property, ready for your CPA.

Investor raise and portal

Investor CRM, subscriptions, e-signature, accreditation checks and a portal.

Unit-level rent data

Deeper multifamily comps once you underwrite every week.

Start with

The essentials for day one. These make up the lean total.

A

Adventures in CRE A.CRE model library

Underwriting

Free, widely used Excel models for acquisitions, development and equity waterfalls. You keep every assumption visible, which investors and lenders expect to see anyway.

FreeFree models, per our listing record; the vendor site blocks automated checks.

Alternatives: TheAnalyst PRO (web-based analysis and marketing reports, $699.99/yr or $89.99/mo); Property Metrics (web-based underwriting, pricing on request)

R

RentCast Pro, billed annually

Market rents

Residential rent estimates with 20 nearby comps per search and portfolio tracking, enough to sanity-check a multifamily rent roll. The free plan (5 comps, 5 properties) may be enough for a single deal. Commercial rents still come from broker comps.

$12/mo$12/mo billed annually ($19 billed monthly), 1 user. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Rentometer (rent comps by address; the vendor site blocks automated checks); HelloData (unit-level multifamily comps, $300 per user per month)

S

Stessa Essentials (free)

Property bookkeeping

Free bank feeds, property-level income and expense tracking and basic reports for each asset. Fund accounting, capital accounts and K-1s still go to your CPA or fund administrator.

FreeEssentials plan, free, unlimited properties. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Baselane (free banking-led bookkeeping; Smart is $20/mo on an annual plan)

Add when

Worth buying once the trigger applies. These, minus anything they replace, make up the fuller total.

C

Covercy Fundraising Starter, 2 entities

Investor raise and portal

Add when you raise from more than about 10 investors, or start a second deal.

Investor CRM and fundraising for 5 team members, with clear per-entity pricing ($249 per entity per month). That pricing adds up with every SPV. Standard ($566/mo for 2 entities) adds distributions and reporting for capital already raised.

$498/mo$249 per entity per month x 2 entities = $498/mo, billed monthly, 5 team members. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Cash Flow Portal (Syndication Suite starts at $699/mo); Agora Real Estate (from $749/mo on a yearly term)

H

HelloData Standard, 1 user

Unit-level rent data

Add when you underwrite multifamily deals most weeks and need unit-level comps and concessions.

Automated rent surveys with unit-level data and rent trends, 10 saved surveys per user. It replaces most of what RentCast does for multifamily, but keep RentCast for single-family and small-property checks if you buy both.

$300/mo$300 per user per month, billed monthly, 1 user. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Rentometer (lighter and cheaper rent comps)

Frequently Asked Questions

What software does a first-time syndicator actually need?

For the first deal, very little: a free Excel model such as the A.CRE library, rent comps (RentCast Pro at $12/mo billed annually), and free bookkeeping such as Stessa Essentials. That lean stack is $12 a month. Your attorney and CPA matter more than software at this stage.

When does an investor portal pay for itself?

Usually once you pass about 10 investors or start a second deal, when subscriptions, accreditation checks, distributions and quarterly reports start to repeat. Covercy's Fundraising Starter is $498/mo for 2 entities at published prices; Cash Flow Portal starts at $699/mo and Agora at $749/mo.

Why is Juniper Square on the skip list?

It is excellent for institutional sponsors but sold by quote and designed for firms running many funds with investor relations staff. The fuller emerging sponsor stack, with a portal and unit-level rent data, comes to $810 a month at published prices.
