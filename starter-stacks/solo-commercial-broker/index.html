Starter Software Stack for a Solo Commercial Broker

You work deals on your own or with a part-time assistant. You need to remember every owner and tenant you talk to, get listings in front of buyers and tenants, and know who owns a building before you call. Most of that starts cheap. The expensive data subscriptions can wait until your pipeline shows you need them.

Who this is for

An independent or newly licensed commercial agent, or a producer at a small shop that does not supply software, working office, retail, industrial or investment sales from a single seat.

Jobs the stack must cover

Contacts and pipeline

Every owner, tenant and deal in one place, with follow-ups tied to properties.

Listing marketing

Flyers, offering memorandums and email blasts that look professional.

Listing exposure

Getting listings in front of buyers and tenant reps where they already search.

Comps

Lease and sale comps to price a listing or a broker opinion of value.

Ownership research

Who owns a building and how to reach them before you prospect.

Start with

The essentials for day one. These make up the lean total.

C

ClientLook (LightBox) Annual plan

Contacts and pipeline

Built for commercial brokers: contacts link to properties, listings and deals, and it publishes its price, which few CRE CRMs do. Month to month is $129 if you are not ready to commit.

$89/mo$1,068 per user per year, paid upfront = $89/mo, 1 seat. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Wise Agent (residential-first, but $42/mo billed annually for up to 5 people); RealNex (CRE CRM priced per user; prices load only in the pricing tool)

C

CREBuilder Starter (free)

Listing marketing

The free plan covers 3 documents with basic templates and online viewing, enough to learn whether its flyers and OMs fit how you sell before you pay.

FreeStarter plan $0, 1 user, 3 documents. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Buildout (the common choice at larger shops, from $125 per user per month); SharpLaunch (property websites, plans from $399/mo on annual billing)

C

Crexi Free listings

Listing exposure

Posting a listing costs nothing and many buyers and tenant reps search here. Add LoopNet's free basic listing for reach; pay for premium placement only on listings that need it.

FreeFree listing tier, per our listing record; the vendor site blocks automated checks.

Alternatives: LoopNet (free basic listings, paid tiers by quote); Brevitas (freemium listing network)

C

Compstak Exchange (free for contributors)

Comps

Brokers and appraisers who contribute their own lease comps earn access to other people's for free. If you will not trade comps, rely on your own deal history and county records until volume justifies a paid source.

FreeFree for contributors, per our listing record; the vendor site blocks automated checks.

Alternatives: Comp Crunch (subscription comps tool); CoStar (the deepest comps database, quote only)

Worth buying once the trigger applies. These, minus anything they replace, make up the fuller total.

Ownership research

Add when you start calling owners outside the buildings you already know.

Recorded owners, property reports and mailing-list exports. Coverage is strongest in New York City and other large metros, so check your county first. Elite ($79.95/mo) and Platinum ($169.95/mo) add more, including real owners behind LLCs in New York City.

$50/mo$599.95 per year billed annually = $50.00/mo, 1 seat; New York State price on the subscriptions page, other regions differ. Checked October 25, 2026 on the vendor pricing page.

Alternatives: Reonomy (owner and debt data built for prospecting at volume, pricing on request); Plotzy (if you broker land or development sites: parcel search by zoning with metered owner contacts, from $180/mo, in beta)

Listing marketing

Full document creation, email marketing and deal rooms on one seat. Monthly billing is $89.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cheapest workable software setup for a solo commercial broker?

About $89 a month at published prices: a commercial CRM (ClientLook at $89/mo billed annually) plus free tools for marketing documents (CREbuilder Starter), listing exposure (Crexi and LoopNet free listings) and comps (CompStak Exchange, if you contribute comps). A residential-first CRM such as Wise Agent, at $42/mo billed annually, brings it lower if you do not need properties tied to contacts.

Does a solo broker need CoStar?

Not to start. CoStar is the most complete comps and listings source, but it is sold by quote by market and seat. Most solo brokers start with free listing marketplaces, comps they trade or collect themselves, and an owner research tool such as PropertyShark, and add CoStar when clients or deal size demand it.

What does the fuller solo broker stack add?

Owner research (PropertyShark Pro, $50/mo billed annually) once you prospect beyond buildings you know, and CREbuilder Pro ($69/mo billed annually) once you outgrow 3 marketing documents. That brings the stack to $208 a month.
