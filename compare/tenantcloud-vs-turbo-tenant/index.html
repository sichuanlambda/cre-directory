Our Verdict

TenantCloud and TurboTenant both target independent landlords who want to list vacancies, screen tenants, sign leases, and collect rent online without a full property management suite. TurboTenant is built around a free core plan and monetizes through tenant-paid screening fees and an optional Premium subscription. TenantCloud is a tiered subscription product that leans further into bookkeeping, owner reporting, and multi-role mobile apps.

TurboTenant wins for landlords who want to pay nothing to get started. Listing syndication, applications, screening, rent collection, and maintenance tracking are all on the free plan. Premium adds state-specific leases, faster payouts, unlimited eSignatures, and priority support; TurboTenant's site lists it from $149 per year for up to 10 units, billed annually only. The caveats are that Premium is priced by unit band, so costs step up as you add doors, and the product is built for owners rather than managers running third-party portfolios.

TenantCloud wins on accounting and structure. Its current pricing page lists Starter at $18 per month, or $15 billed annually, with unlimited units but a cap of 10 leases; Growth, Pro, and Business raise the lease cap and add lead texting, a lease builder, inspections, and state-specific forms. Separate apps for landlords, tenants, owners, and service providers make it workable for a small manager with a few owner clients. QuickBooks Online sync is a real advantage over TurboTenant for anyone who works with an accountant. The trade-offs are that no free tier appears on the current pricing page and ACH payment fees apply per transaction.

Pick TurboTenant if you self-manage a handful of units and want free core tools. Pick TenantCloud if you need proper books, an owner portal, or QuickBooks sync and are willing to pay a modest monthly fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is TenantCloud free?

Not on its current pricing page. TenantCloud lists its Starter plan at $18 per month, or $15 per month billed annually, limited to 10 active leases. TurboTenant is the one with a free core plan.

How much does TurboTenant Premium cost?

TurboTenant lists Premium from $149 per year for landlords with 1 to 10 units, with higher annual prices for larger unit bands. Billing is annual only, with no month-to-month option.

Which is better for a small property management company?

TenantCloud. It offers an owner portal, a separate owner app, QuickBooks Online sync, and a Business tier with unlimited leases. TurboTenant is designed for owners managing their own rentals.

Do TenantCloud and TurboTenant both syndicate listings?

Yes. TurboTenant pushes listings to Zillow, Trulia, HotPads, and Realtor.com, while TenantCloud syndicates to Apartments.com, Realtor.com, and Rentler.
