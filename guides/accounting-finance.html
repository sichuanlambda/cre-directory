BUYER'S GUIDE

Best CRE Accounting & Finance Software (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 12 min read

TL;DR

CRE accounting isn't one product — it's four: property accounting (Yardi, MRI, Re-Leased, STRATAFOLIO), lease accounting compliance (Visual Lease, FinQuery), and fund/investor accounting (Juniper Square). Yardi Breeze's $1/unit/month is the only published price in the bunch. Match the tool to which books you're actually closing.

QuickBooks runs more commercial real estate than anyone wants to admit. It works right up until it doesn't: CAM reconciliation season, an ASC 842 audit, or the first investor who asks for a proper capital account statement. That's usually the moment operators go shopping for the tools below.

Our directory lists 37 products in this category, and it's a grab bag — lending marketplaces, insurance products, e-signature tools, and payment processors all technically live here. We filtered down to the 7 that do actual accounting and financial management work for CRE: keeping property-level books, closing compliance gaps, or running investor money.

Things to know before the list:

  • Figure out which books you're closing. Property-level P&Ls, ASC 842 balance sheet compliance, and fund-level investor accounting are three different jobs. Nobody on this list does all three well.
  • Integration is the trap buyers underestimate. Your accounting system has to talk to your PM platform, your bank, and your tax preparers. A great standalone tool that requires manual re-entry creates more problems than it solves.
  • Migration is painful, so pick for 3-5 years. Moving accounting data between systems is one of the worst projects in CRE ops. Buy the tool you can grow into, not just the one that fits today.

The 7 Best Accounting & Finance Tools for CRE

1. Yardi

Best for: One accounting stack across any portfolio

Full Profile →

Yardi's accounting is the reason many operators tolerate everything else about Yardi: a single stack where the general ledger, property management, and investment management all share the same data. It serves every property type, and Yardi Breeze gives smaller operators a genuinely affordable entry at $1/unit/month ($2 for Breeze Premier), with Voyager handling enterprise portfolios on quote-based pricing.

The honest downsides: Voyager is complex to configure, implementations run months, some module interfaces feel dated, and once your books live in Yardi, leaving is hard. That data lock-in is also, from Yardi's perspective, the business model.

What's good

  • Most comprehensive single-stack platform in CRE
  • Serves all property types, in 80+ countries
  • Breeze is an affordable entry point for small operators
  • AI workflow automation via Yardi Virtuoso

What's not

  • Voyager is complex with a steep learning curve
  • Implementations take months, not weeks
  • Some legacy interfaces feel dated
  • Deep data lock-in makes migrating away hard

Pricing: Yardi Breeze from $1/unit/month; Breeze Premier from $2/unit/month; Voyager quote-based.

2. MRI Software

Best for: Enterprises that want an open platform, not a walled garden

Full Profile →

MRI is Yardi's perennial rival and the philosophical opposite on one axis: openness. Its platform connects to 200+ integration partners, so you can run MRI accounting alongside best-of-breed tools instead of buying everything from one vendor. With a 50-year track record and 50,000+ clients globally, it covers commercial and residential property accounting, lease management, and investment management.

The cost of that flexibility is sprawl — years of acquisitions mean product experiences vary — plus enterprise pricing, a steep learning curve, and implementation timelines that can stretch.

What's good

  • Comprehensive platform across commercial, residential, investment
  • Open platform with 200+ integration partners
  • Longest track record in CRE technology
  • Global reach, 50,000+ clients

What's not

  • Complex, with a steep learning curve
  • Enterprise pricing, not transparent
  • Acquisition sprawl creates inconsistent UX
  • Lengthy implementations

Pricing: Quote-based, annual. Contact sales.

3. Visual Lease

Best for: Enterprise lease accounting and lifecycle management

Full Profile →

Visual Lease pairs 25+ years of lease management depth with full ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87 compliance, covering the entire lease lifecycle from acquisition to termination for both real estate and equipment leases. It's built for Fortune 500-scale portfolios where real estate, finance, procurement, and legal all need to work from the same lease data, with SOX-grade controls. A 97%+ customer retention rate is about as strong a signal as this market produces.

It was recently acquired by CoStar Group — strong backing, but watch how pricing and product direction evolve. It's enterprise-only, implementations for global portfolios take time, and some screens are still being modernized.

What's good

  • Deepest lease-management domain expertise in the market
  • 97%+ customer retention
  • Handles real estate and equipment leases together
  • Purpose-built SOX controls and multi-team collaboration

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only — not for small businesses
  • Lengthy implementation for complex global portfolios
  • Some screens feel dated; UI work ongoing
  • CoStar acquisition may change pricing/direction

Pricing: Enterprise, annual. Contact sales.

4. LeaseQuery (FinQuery)

Best for: Lease accounting compliance at any company size

Full Profile →

LeaseQuery — now rebranded FinQuery — is the accountant's pick for ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87 compliance: automated lease data management, journal entries, and disclosure reporting. It leads G2 rankings across small, mid-market, and enterprise segments with top satisfaction scores for support and ease of use, and it has extended beyond leases into prepaids and accruals, which broadens the value for finance teams.

It's a compliance tool, not an operational lease manager — critical dates and transaction workflows aren't the point — and the rebrand creates some market confusion. Pricing is quote-only.

What's good

  • G2 leader across all company-size segments
  • Top scores for support quality and ease of use
  • Covers ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87
  • Extends to prepaids and accruals

What's not

  • Pricing not public — must contact sales
  • Compliance-focused; limited operational lease management
  • LeaseQuery-to-FinQuery rebrand causes confusion
  • Not a full property management platform

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact sales.

5. Juniper Square

Best for: GPs managing funds and investor capital

Full Profile →

Juniper Square handles the money side that property accounting systems don't: fundraising, investor onboarding, compliance, treasury, and reporting for private-markets GPs, with fund administration services layered on top of the software. It serves over 2,000 GPs representing $1 trillion in investor equity, and its JunieAI assistant is built into the platform for workflow automation and insight.

If you don't manage outside capital, this isn't your tool — it's fund infrastructure, not property bookkeeping. Pricing scales with AUM and requirements, smaller managers may find it rich, and migrating off legacy fund-admin setups is a real project.

What's good

  • Unified platform for every GP and LP workflow
  • Handles complex fund structures with precision
  • 2,000+ GPs, $1T in investor equity
  • JunieAI automation built in

What's not

  • Fund-focused, not direct property management
  • Pricing may be high for smaller fund managers
  • Complex implementation from legacy systems
  • Real change management required

Pricing: Quote-based, priced on AUM and requirements.

6. Re-Leased

Best for: Commercial landlords who love Xero or QuickBooks

Full Profile →

Re-Leased is cloud-native commercial property management with the financial plumbing done right: excellent Xero and QuickBooks integrations, automated rent collection, and — the features commercial operators actually need — automated rent reviews, CPI adjustments, and turnover rent calculations. It was designed for office, retail, industrial, and mixed-use from day one, not adapted from a residential product.

Its US market presence is smaller than Yardi's or MRI's, pricing (per-property) isn't published, and residential functionality is limited. For a small-to-mid commercial portfolio that wants to keep its general ledger in Xero or QuickBooks, it's arguably the cleanest option here.

What's good

  • Purpose-built for commercial, not adapted residential
  • Excellent Xero and QuickBooks integrations
  • Automated rent reviews and CPI adjustments
  • Modern cloud-native platform with good UX

What's not

  • Smaller US presence than Yardi or MRI
  • Pricing not publicly listed
  • Limited residential functionality
  • Fewer integrations than the big platforms

Pricing: Per-property, monthly or annual. Not publicly listed. Free trial available.

7. STRATAFOLIO

Best for: QuickBooks users who aren't ready to leave QuickBooks

Full Profile →

STRATAFOLIO takes the opposite bet from everyone above: instead of replacing QuickBooks, it wraps CRE-specific tools around it. Deep integration with QuickBooks Online and Desktop adds lease management, automated invoicing and reminders, payments, and investor reporting on top of the accounting system a small commercial owner already runs. For the "QuickBooks plus spreadsheets" operator, it's the lowest-disruption upgrade on this list.

The dependency is the point and the limitation — no QuickBooks, no STRATAFOLIO — and its feature depth doesn't compare to Yardi or MRI. That's fine; neither does its complexity.

What's good

  • Best QuickBooks integration for commercial real estate
  • Purpose-built for commercial, not residential adapted
  • Keep QuickBooks while adding CRE tools
  • Automated invoicing and reminders; free trial

What's not

  • Requires QuickBooks — no other accounting systems
  • Smaller company, less feature depth than Yardi/MRI
  • Not a full property management platform
  • Few integrations beyond the QuickBooks ecosystem

Pricing: Subscription, monthly or annual. Not publicly listed. Free trial available.

Read the "Job" column first. Yardi vs. MRI is a real head-to-head; so is Visual Lease vs. FinQuery. But comparing Juniper Square to STRATAFOLIO is comparing a fund administrator to a QuickBooks plugin — different problems, different buyers, and quite possibly both in the same company's stack.

The bottom line

Small commercial portfolio on QuickBooks? Try STRATAFOLIO or Re-Leased before committing to an enterprise migration — both have free trials, which is rare here. Growing operator ready to consolidate? The Yardi vs. MRI decision comes down to single-stack convenience vs. open-platform flexibility, and either way you're signing up for a serious implementation. If ASC 842 is your specific pain, FinQuery (broad market) or Visual Lease (enterprise lease portfolios) will solve it without replacing your whole stack. And if you manage investor capital, Juniper Square is solving a problem none of the others touch. Whatever you pick, map your integrations before you sign — the tool is only as good as the data flowing into it.
