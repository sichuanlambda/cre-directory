BUYER'S GUIDE

Best Legal & Compliance Software for CRE (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

TL;DR

Legal and compliance in CRE isn't one product category — it's lease accounting compliance (Visual Lease, FinQuery), zoning due diligence (Zoneomics, from $25/report), permitting (OpenCounter, if your city has it), and transaction management with eSignatures (Dotloop, from $31.99/month). Most firms need one tool from one or two of those buckets, not a suite.

"Legal and compliance software" for commercial real estate is a small category with an identity crisis. Nobody sells a single platform that handles your ASC 842 disclosures, checks the zoning on an acquisition, and gets your leases signed. What exists instead is a handful of specialized tools, each covering one legal risk that used to require an attorney's time or a paralegal's spreadsheet.

Our directory tracks 8 products in this category. Several are really adjacent tools — ESG reporting platforms and hospitality payment systems that touch compliance without being about it. We cut to the 5 that a CRE operator, corporate real estate team, or brokerage would actually shortlist for a legal or compliance problem.

Things to know before the list:

  • Know which compliance problem you're solving. Lease accounting standards (ASC 842, IFRS 16, GASB 87) are a finance problem. Zoning is a due diligence problem. eSignatures and audit trails are a transaction problem. The tools don't overlap much.
  • Pricing transparency is rare here. Only Zoneomics and Dotloop publish meaningful numbers. The enterprise lease accounting platforms all want you on a sales call.
  • Software reduces legal risk; it doesn't replace counsel. These tools catch problems earlier and cheaper. The problems they catch still often end up on a lawyer's desk.

The 5 Best Legal & Compliance Tools for CRE

1. Visual Lease

Best for: Enterprise lease compliance and lifecycle management

Full Profile →

Visual Lease is the enterprise pick for lease compliance: end-to-end lease lifecycle management combined with accounting compliance for ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87, plus purpose-built SOX controls. With 25+ years in lease management, it serves Fortune 500 companies, unifying real estate, finance, procurement, and legal teams around centralized lease data — real estate and equipment leases both. Its 97%+ customer retention says a lot about how sticky it is once implemented.

It was recently acquired by CoStar Group, which brings strong backing but also some uncertainty about future pricing and direction. This is enterprise software in every sense: quote-only pricing, potentially lengthy implementations for global portfolios, and a UI that's still mid-modernization in places.

What's good

  • 25+ years of lease management domain expertise
  • 97%+ customer retention
  • SOX-ready controls, multi-team collaboration
  • Real estate and equipment leases in one platform

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only — not for small businesses
  • Implementations can run long for complex portfolios
  • Some screens feel dated
  • CoStar acquisition may change pricing/direction

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact for pricing.

2. LeaseQuery (FinQuery)

Best for: Lease accounting compliance at any company size

Full Profile →

LeaseQuery — now rebranded as FinQuery — is the accountant's counterpart to Visual Lease. It automates lease data management, journal entries, disclosure reporting, and compliance workflows for ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87, and it's a G2 category leader across small business, mid-market, and enterprise segments with top satisfaction scores for support and ease of use. It also extends beyond leases into prepaids and accruals.

The focus is squarely on accounting compliance rather than operational lease management — if you need critical-date tracking, options management, and portfolio operations, Visual Lease covers more ground. And the LeaseQuery-to-FinQuery rebrand still confuses buyers searching for it.

What's good

  • G2 leader from small business through enterprise
  • Covers ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87
  • Top-rated support and implementation accuracy
  • Extends to prepaids and accruals

What's not

  • No public pricing — sales call required
  • Limited operational lease management
  • FinQuery rebrand causes market confusion

Pricing: Quote-based; contact sales.

3. Zoneomics

Best for: Zoning due diligence and site search

Full Profile →

Zoneomics digitizes and aggregates zoning and land-use data from thousands of fragmented city sources into the largest nationwide zoning dataset on the market, updated weekly, with AI-driven analysis that translates dense zoning codes into something you can act on. Its users include Redfin, Cushman & Wakefield, and Moody's, and an API lets you pipe zoning data into your own platform.

It's also the rare tool here with transparent entry pricing: basic zoning search is free, and reports start at $25. The caveat is coverage — not every jurisdiction is fully digitized, so verify your target market before relying on it, and certified zoning letters for closings can get expensive on a per-transaction basis.

What's good

  • Largest nationwide zoning data coverage
  • Used by Redfin, Cushman & Wakefield, Moody's
  • Weekly data updates
  • Free search tier; API for integrations

What's not

  • Coverage varies by city and state
  • Certified zoning letters can be pricey per deal
  • Free search is limited without paid reports

Pricing: Free zoning search. Reports from $25. API access: contact for pricing.

4. Dotloop

Best for: Transaction management with compliant eSignatures

Full Profile →

Dotloop, a Zillow Group company, handles the transaction-compliance side: document editing, legally binding eSignatures with a comprehensive audit trail, and compliance tracking from offer to close. It's used by hundreds of thousands of agents, with 190+ MLS and association partnerships for forms coverage and 75+ integrations, and the Broker tier adds a compliance dashboard for reviewing agent transactions.

The honest framing: Dotloop's DNA is residential — the MLS forms libraries and Zillow ownership tell you who it was built for. For CRE brokerages it works as an affordable eSignature and transaction-audit layer at $31.99/month per agent, but it isn't going to abstract your NNN leases.

What's good

  • Legally binding eSignatures with full audit trail
  • Industry-standard, trusted by hundreds of thousands of agents
  • 75+ integrations; 190+ MLS/association partnerships
  • Published entry pricing

What's not

  • Residential-first heritage; forms coverage is MLS-centric
  • SMS signing and task templates need paid upgrades
  • Team/brokerage pricing not published
  • Interface can feel dated

Pricing: Agent plan $31.99/month (unlimited transactions, eSignatures, audit trail). Teams and Broker tiers: contact for pricing.

5. OpenCounter

Best for: Permitting and zoning clearance — where cities have adopted it

Full Profile →

OpenCounter is the odd one out on this list: it's sold to governments, not to you. Cities deploy it to put zoning verification, permit applications, and business licensing online, with GIS integration and transparent fee estimates. It's live in 40+ US cities including Orlando, Atlanta, Indianapolis, Salt Lake City, Colorado Springs, and Miami, and it dramatically cuts permitting processing times where it's in place.

For developers and tenants, that means free self-service clarity on what a parcel allows and what approvals will cost — but only in participating cities. You can't subscribe to it directly; you can only hope (or lobby) your jurisdiction adopts it.

What's good

  • Deployed in 40+ US cities
  • Transparent zoning and fee info for developers
  • Dramatically faster permit processing
  • GIS-integrated property and zoning data

What's not

  • Only useful where a municipality has adopted it
  • B2G model — no direct individual subscriptions

Pricing: Municipal annual license; contact for pricing. Free for end users in participating cities.

Notice there's essentially no overlap between these five. The only head-to-head matchup is Visual Lease vs. FinQuery, and even that splits cleanly: Visual Lease if legal and real estate teams need to operate the lease portfolio, FinQuery if the accounting team just needs clean, compliant numbers.

The Bottom Line

Buy for the risk that actually keeps your team up at night. Public-company or audited operators need lease accounting software first — Visual Lease for operational depth, FinQuery for accounting-led teams. Acquirers and developers should put Zoneomics in the due diligence workflow, since a $25 report is the cheapest zoning insurance you'll ever buy, and check whether target cities run OpenCounter before assuming permitting timelines. Brokerages that just need signatures and an audit trail can start with Dotloop at $31.99/month. None of it replaces your attorney — but all of it makes the billable hours count for more.
