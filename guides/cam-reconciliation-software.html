BUYER'S GUIDE

Best CAM Reconciliation Software for Commercial Landlords

Updated October 2026 · 12 min read

TL;DR

Rent Manager has the most fully documented CAM reconciliation: caps, base amounts, admin fees, five ways to share expenses and a tenant statement. MRI Software suits institutional portfolios with many recovery pools. Yardi Breeze Premier and Re-Leased cover caps and expense pools for small and mid-size commercial managers, and STRATAFOLIO is the pick if your books live in QuickBooks, with published pricing. DoorLoop and Property Matrix offer CAM inside broader platforms at published per-unit prices. Few vendors document gross-ups, so test one of your own leases before you buy.

Every January, commercial landlords start the same job: total up last year's operating expenses, work out what each tenant actually owes under its lease, compare that with the monthly estimates the tenant already paid, and send a bill or a credit with enough backup to survive a tenant audit. Done in spreadsheets, each lease's caps, base year, exclusions and gross-up clause become a separate formula that someone has to maintain. This guide compares the software that runs CAM reconciliations for landlords, and notes the separate tools tenants use to check them.

What a CAM Reconciliation Is, and When It Happens

Under a net lease, a tenant pays monthly estimates of its share of common area maintenance and other operating expenses, set from the landlord's budget. After the year closes, the landlord compares actual recoverable expenses with what was billed and bills or refunds the difference. A Wiss article from May 2026 puts the usual window at 90 to 180 days after the close of the calendar year, as specified in the lease [1], which for calendar-year leases means the first or second quarter. IREM notes that some leases set a hard deadline, and a landlord that misses it may have to absorb the costs [2]. That makes the first half of the year the busy season for this software.

What to Look For

  • Pro-rata share. The tenant's share is usually its square footage divided by a denominator: the whole building, the leased area, or a pool that excludes anchors or certain units. Check that the software can hold a different denominator per pool and per lease.
  • Caps. Leases may cap increases in controllable expenses, cumulatively or not, and exclude uncontrollable costs such as taxes and insurance. Ask how the tool stores the cap type and carries unused cap forward.
  • Base year or expense stop. Office leases often bill only increases over a base year or a fixed stop. The tool should subtract the base before allocating.
  • Gross-up. When a building is partly empty, gross-up clauses restate costs that vary with occupancy (janitorial, utilities, management fees) as if the building were 95% or 100% occupied, so early tenants do not pay for vacant space; fixed costs such as taxes and insurance are not grossed up [3][4]. This is the feature vendors document least.
  • Admin fees and exclusions. A management or admin fee on recoverable costs, and lease-specific exclusions, both need to live on the lease record, not in a spreadsheet.
  • Statements and audit trail. Tenants increasingly audit reconciliations. Look for a tenant-facing statement that shows the pool, the share, the cap and the math, with the invoices behind it.
  • Next year's estimates. The reconciliation should feed new monthly estimates without re-keying.

The 7 Best CAM Reconciliation Tools for Landlords

1. Rent Manager

Best for: Mid-size commercial and mixed portfolios that want the reconciliation rules documented in detail

Full Profile →

Rent Manager has the most thoroughly documented CAM reconciliation of the tools we checked, and its help site is public. You choose the properties, charge types, expense accounts, date range and cash or accrual basis; the system applies each lease's caps per charge type (posting the capped amount), subtracts base amounts before distributing expenses, prorates for move-ins and move-outs, takes manual add or deduct adjustments, and posts each tenant's true-up as a charge or a credit [5]. Expenses can be shared by percentage of total square feet, percentage of rented square feet, per occupant, evenly, or by a lease-defined pro-rata share [6], and lease fields cover base year, cap increase percentage, cap term and administrative fee percentage [7]. A CAM Reconciliation Statement report is built to be sent to commercial tenants [8].

Next year's estimates can be set from last year's expenses or budget with a percentage increase. Two gaps: we found no documented gross-up to a target occupancy (the rented square feet method is an occupancy-based denominator, which is not the same thing), and no distinction between cumulative and non-cumulative caps. Rent Manager sells Basic, Plus and Premium bundles by quote.

What's good

  • Public, detailed help documentation for every reconciliation step
  • Caps, base amounts, admin fees and move-in/move-out proration
  • Five ways to share expenses, including a lease-defined share
  • Tenant-facing reconciliation statement report

What's not

  • No published pricing
  • Gross-up to a target occupancy not documented
  • Cumulative vs non-cumulative cap handling not documented

Pricing: Basic, Plus and Premium bundles; quote on request.

2. MRI Software

Best for: Institutional retail, office and industrial landlords with complex recovery pools

Full Profile →

MRI Commercial Management handles recoveries for large commercial portfolios: budgeted expense pools drive monthly estimate billing and estimate letters to tenants, and the year-end reconciliation, which MRI says is typically run a few months after year-end close, posts each tenant's result to its ledger as a one-time charge or credit [9]. Retail landlords can add a recoveries module alongside Commercial Management. In MRI's Urban Edge case study, recoveries handle different admin fees and cap types for CAM, tax and capital expense pools, with invoice backup for tenants [10].

That case study is also a caveat: Urban Edge's setup needed custom formula work from an implementation partner, which is typical for portfolios with many lease variations. MRI's public pages do not document gross-up, base year or exclusion handling, so test those on your own leases in a demo. MRI also sells CAM recoveries as a managed service if you would rather outsource the work. Pricing is by quote.

What's good

  • Built for large, complex commercial portfolios
  • Expense pools drive both estimates and the year-end true-up
  • Admin fees and multiple cap types across CAM, tax and capex pools
  • Optional managed recoveries service

What's not

  • No published pricing
  • Complex setups may need partner configuration
  • Gross-up and base year handling not documented publicly

Pricing: Quote on request.

3. Yardi Breeze Premier

Best for: Small and mid-size commercial managers who want published pricing from a large vendor

Full Profile →

Yardi's Breeze line is the self-serve side of Yardi, and both Breeze and Breeze Premier run recovery reconciliations [11]. Commercial features include NNN, gross and combination leases, custom expense pools allocated to tenants, CAM schedules, caps and percentage allocations for expense pools, and estimated charges posted for monthly, quarterly or annual reconciliation [12]. At year-end a reconciliation report creates the charges or credits, and a reconciliation letter shows each tenant the balance and calculation detail and can be emailed [13]. Premier adds charges by amount per area, where Breeze allows only flat-rate CAM charges.

Pricing is published: commercial Breeze is $2 per unit per month with a $200 monthly minimum on an annual agreement, and Breeze Premier for commercial is quoted [14]. The CommercialCafe tenant portal is a paid Premier add-on. Yardi's public Breeze pages do not document gross-up, base year or admin fee handling. Larger portfolios usually look at Yardi Voyager Commercial instead; Yardi's site blocked our automated checks, so we have not described Voyager here.

What's good

  • Recovery reconciliations in both Breeze and Breeze Premier
  • Expense pools with caps and percentage allocations
  • Reconciliation letters with calculation detail, sent by email
  • Published commercial pricing for Breeze

What's not

  • Breeze Premier commercial pricing by quote
  • Gross-up, base year and admin fee handling not documented
  • Tenant portal is a paid add-on

Pricing: Commercial Breeze $2/unit/month, $200/month minimum, annual agreement; Breeze Premier (commercial) by quote.

4. Re-Leased

Best for: Commercial owners and managers who keep their general ledger in Xero, QuickBooks Online, Sage Intacct or NetSuite

Full Profile →

Re-Leased is commercial property management software with CAM budgets and annual reconciliation on its Core plan. Expenses can be apportioned by square footage or custom percentages, and the budget tools handle expense caps, collars, fixed amounts, base-year stops and tenant-specific exclusions, prorate automatically for mid-year move-ins and move-outs, and generate balancing charges and tenant statements [15]. Budget-to-actual variance alerts flag pools that are running over during the year.

It syncs two ways with Xero, QuickBooks Online, Sage Intacct and NetSuite, so it suits teams that want a commercial-first operations tool without replacing their accounting system. Gross-ups and admin fees are not documented on its product pages. All three plans (Core, Pro and Enterprise, the last for more than 1,000 tenancies) are priced on request [16].

What's good

  • Caps, collars, base-year stops and tenant-specific exclusions
  • Balancing charges and tenant statements generated for you
  • Variance alerts during the year
  • Two-way sync with four common general ledgers

What's not

  • No published pricing
  • Gross-up and admin fee handling not documented
  • Less depth for very large institutional portfolios than MRI or Yardi

Pricing: Core, Pro and Enterprise; pricing on request.

5. STRATAFOLIO

Best for: Small commercial landlords who run their books in QuickBooks

Full Profile →

STRATAFOLIO adds commercial property management, including CAM reconciliation, on top of QuickBooks. Its knowledge base walks through a full reconciliation: pro-rata shares from unit versus building square footage with a lease override, caps with a controllable and non-controllable split, a base year or expense stop review, admin and management fees where the lease allows them, delivery of statements by email, mail or the tenant portal, and resetting next year's estimates by cloning the operating expense setup [17].

The same page is candid about limits: STRATAFOLIO runs on a cash basis, cannot exclude individual transaction lines (so reimbursable expenses need their own accounts), and overpayment credit memos are created in QuickBooks. Gross-ups are not covered. Pricing is published and billed annually: CAM reconciliation starts on the Professional plan at $190 a month for the first five units plus $4 per additional unit, with a one-time onboarding fee [18].

What's good

  • Published pricing
  • Clear, step-by-step reconciliation documentation
  • Controllable vs non-controllable caps, base year and admin fees
  • Keeps QuickBooks as the system of record

What's not

  • Cash basis only
  • No gross-up support documented
  • Credit memos and line-level exclusions need QuickBooks workarounds

Pricing: Professional (includes CAM reconciliation) $190/month plus $4 per unit above 5; Enterprise $230/month plus $6 per unit; billed annually, plus onboarding.

6. DoorLoop

Best for: Mixed residential and commercial managers who want one modern system at a published per-unit price

Full Profile →

DoorLoop is mainly known for residential management, but it markets a commercial module with CAM charges and a CAM reconciliation report. DoorLoop says the report pulls actuals from your profit and loss statement, calculates each tenant's pro-rata share, applies per-lease admin fees and base year stops, and exports tenant statement PDFs; CAM charges can be posted as recurring or bulk charges by square footage with true-ups calculated automatically [19].

That description comes from DoorLoop's marketing and FAQ pages; we found no help-center article showing the mechanics, and caps, gross-ups and exclusions are not mentioned, so ask for a demo on a lease that has them. Pricing is per unit and published: from $2.50 per unit per month billed yearly with a $199 monthly minimum [20].

What's good

  • Published per-unit pricing with unlimited users
  • Pro-rata share, admin fees and base year stops in the reconciliation report
  • Tenant statement PDFs
  • Same system for residential and commercial units

What's not

  • Mechanics documented only at FAQ level
  • Caps and gross-ups not mentioned
  • $199 monthly minimum

Pricing: From $2.50/unit/month billed yearly ($199/month minimum); $3.00/unit monthly.

7. Property Matrix

Best for: Managers who want formula-level control over how CAM is calculated

Full Profile →

Property Matrix builds CAM charges from formula-based charge structures: pro-rata share as unit area over building area, cap and minimum logic, and denominators that can exclude flagged units. A designated item records CAM already paid so the reconciliation can deduct it, and vendor bills that match a structure can invoice tenants automatically as pass-through expenses [21].

Budgets, CAM charges and reconciliation sit in its Operations and Finance Plus pack, which is not part of the Starter plan. Its help center does not walk through year-end reconciliation, base years, gross-ups or tenant statements. Pricing is published: Standard is $350 a month for 100 units with any three packs, and commercial units count as three residential units [22].

What's good

  • Formula-based CAM structures with caps and minimums
  • Denominators can exclude flagged units
  • Pass-through bills can invoice tenants automatically
  • Published plan pricing

What's not

  • CAM requires a paid feature pack
  • Year-end reconciliation steps not documented publicly
  • Commercial units count as three units toward plan limits

Pricing: Standard $350/month (100 units, any 3 packs); Pro $550/month (250 units, all 5 packs); Starter $150/month has no packs.

Also Worth Knowing

  • Sage Intacct Real Estate, an add-on subscription for Sage Intacct, applies stops, caps and base year amounts to recoveries, and its 2026 Release 3 added gross-up calculations for variable expenses when occupancy is below a target, item-level caps and reconciliation previews [23]. It is the only mid-market product we found that documents gross-ups publicly. Sage's main site blocked our checks, so we could not confirm how this relates to Sage 300 Construction and Real Estate.
  • AppFolio says its Core plan includes CAM tracking and reconciliation, but its public pages do not describe the mechanics, so ask for a demo on a capped lease. Pricing starts at $1.49 per unit per month with a 50-unit minimum.
  • Buildium handles CAM through expense allocations and bulk charges; its help article has you compare budget totals with actuals and post bulk charges or credits by hand, with no caps or base years. It fits associations and light commercial use better than multi-tenant retail or office.
  • Prophia does not calculate reconciliations, but it abstracts the lease terms they depend on (caps on controllable expenses, base years, gross-ups, pro-rata shares and exclusions) and integrates with Yardi and MRI, which helps when lease data is the bottleneck.
  • Very large owners on Oracle JD Edwards or SAP can use their real estate modules (JD Edwards Real Estate Management and SAP RE-FX service charge settlement), which document cumulative caps, gross-ups and admin fees in detail but are enterprise ERP projects.

On the Tenant Side: Checking a Landlord's Reconciliation

Tenants with many locations use lease administration software to check the statements landlords send. Visual Lease (now part of CoStar) audits operating expense, tax and CAM invoices and reconciliations against lease terms; Nakisa reconciles landlord year-end invoices against pro-rata shares and caps; Occupier stores landlord statements and sends reconciliation deadline alerts for retail tenants; Tango offers a CAM and operating expense reconciliation review; and Accruent's Lucernex compares estimates with actuals as statements arrive. If you are a landlord, expect your larger tenants to be using one of these.

How to Choose

Start with where your books live and how complicated your leases are.

  • Choose Rent Manager if you want the reconciliation rules documented in detail and a statement built for tenants, across a mixed or commercial portfolio.
  • Choose MRI Software for institutional portfolios with many recovery pools and lease variations, and budget for implementation work.
  • Choose Yardi Breeze Premier if you want a large vendor with published entry pricing and expect to stay small or mid-size.
  • Choose Re-Leased if you are commercial-only and want to keep Xero, QuickBooks Online, Sage Intacct or NetSuite as your ledger.
  • Choose STRATAFOLIO if you already run QuickBooks and have a modest portfolio, and can live with cash-basis reconciliations.
  • Choose DoorLoop or Property Matrix if CAM is one part of a mostly residential or mixed business and published pricing matters.
  • Whatever you pick, run one real lease with a cap, a base year and a gross-up clause through the demo, and compare the result with last year's spreadsheet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a CAM reconciliation?

It is the year-end comparison between the common area maintenance and operating expense estimates a commercial tenant paid during the year and its actual share of those costs under its lease. The landlord bills the shortfall or credits the overpayment, usually with a statement showing the expense pools, the tenant's share and any caps or base year.

When are CAM reconciliations done?

After the year closes. A May 2026 Wiss article describes the usual window as 90 to 180 days after the close of the calendar year, as the lease specifies, so the first half of the year for calendar-year leases. Some leases set a firm deadline, so check yours.

What is a gross-up in a commercial lease?

A clause that restates expenses that vary with occupancy, such as janitorial and utilities, as if the building were 95% or 100% occupied, so tenants in a partly empty building do not pay for vacant space. Fixed costs like taxes and insurance are not grossed up. Few CAM tools document gross-up support, so test it in a demo.

Can I do CAM reconciliations in QuickBooks?

Not natively. STRATAFOLIO adds CAM reconciliation on top of QuickBooks, with caps, base years and admin fees, though it works on a cash basis. Re-Leased also syncs with QuickBooks Online.

How much does CAM reconciliation software cost?

Rent Manager, MRI, Re-Leased and Yardi Breeze Premier quote on request. Published prices start at $2 per unit per month for commercial Yardi Breeze ($200 minimum), $2.50 per unit per month for DoorLoop ($199 minimum), $190 a month plus $4 per extra unit for STRATAFOLIO's Professional plan, and $350 a month for Property Matrix Standard.
