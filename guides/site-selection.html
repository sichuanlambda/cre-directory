BUYER'S GUIDE

Best Site Selection Software for CRE (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 11 min read

TL;DR

Site selection software answers three different questions: where are the people (Placer.ai, SiteZeus, SiteSeer), what can I legally build (Zoneomics, LandVision), and does the deal pencil (TestFit). No single tool answers all three. Free tiers from Placer.ai and Zoneomics are the cheapest way to start; everything else is quote-based.

A bad location can kill a well-run business, and no amount of operational skill fixes it afterward. That's why serious operators stopped relying on windshield surveys and gut feel. The tools below model trade areas, forecast revenue, and check zoning before anyone gets on a plane.

The category is messier than it looks, though. "Site selection software" gets used for retail analytics platforms, parcel mapping tools, zoning databases, and development feasibility software — and those solve genuinely different problems. We went through the 16 tools in our directory, dropped the ones that have been absorbed into other companies or that we can't verify are still operating, and kept 7 that each earn their spot for a distinct job.

A few things to know before the list:

  • Match the tool to the question. A restaurant chain choosing its next market needs foot traffic and demographics. A developer sizing a parcel needs zoning and feasibility modeling. Buying the wrong category of tool is the most common mistake here.
  • Validate the data against sites you know. Before signing anything annual, run 5-10 of your existing locations through the platform and check its numbers against your ground truth. Data quality varies a lot by market.
  • Pricing is almost all quote-based. Placer.ai and Zoneomics have usable free tiers; nearly everything else requires a sales call.

The 7 Best Site Selection Tools for CRE

1. Placer.ai

Best for: Foot traffic and trade area analysis

Full Profile →

Placer.ai is the name everyone in retail CRE already knows, and for good reason: granular foot traffic data, visitor demographics, trade area analysis, and competitive benchmarking for essentially any physical location in the US. The free dashboard is genuinely useful for quick lookups, which is rare in this category, and the paid tiers add full analytics, custom reports, and API access for data feeds.

Know its limits. The data comes from a mobile panel, so certain demographics can be underrepresented, coverage is US-focused, and if you're in pure office or industrial, foot traffic just matters less to you.

What's good

  • Free dashboard provides real value for quick lookups
  • 92%+ claimed accuracy with strong mobile data methodology
  • Foot traffic data traditional CRE providers don't have
  • Strong API for custom analytics workflows

What's not

  • US-focused — limited international coverage
  • Premium pricing not public and can be expensive
  • Mobile panel may underrepresent some demographics
  • Less relevant for pure office or industrial

Pricing: Free tier available. Professional and Enterprise (API, data feeds): contact for pricing.

2. SiteZeus

Best for: Multi-unit brands planning expansion

Full Profile →

SiteZeus covers the whole location lifecycle for multi-unit brands: market planning, franchise sales, site scoring, AI-driven revenue forecasting, and even a construction management module to track the development pipeline. If you're a fast-food, convenience, or retail chain opening locations on a schedule, this is the category of tool built for you.

The flip side of "end-to-end for chains" is that it's overkill for anyone picking one site. It also works best when you feed it your own sales data — the forecasting models need something to learn from.

What's good

  • End-to-end platform from planning to operations
  • AI-driven revenue forecasting reduces expansion risk
  • Serves all business verticals
  • Construction module tracks the development pipeline

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing not publicly available
  • Built for multi-unit brands, not single-site operators
  • Needs data integration for maximum accuracy

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact sales.

3. SiteSeer

Best for: Retail analytics without the enterprise learning curve

Full Profile →

SiteSeer was designed by retail analysts, and it shows: consumer analysis, sales forecasting, competitive intelligence, void analysis, and territory planning, packaged in an interface that doesn't require a GIS degree. Brands like Mattress Firm and i9 Sports use it, and the void analysis tools — figuring out which tenants a trade area is missing — are useful for landlords and brokers, not just occupiers.

It's retail-first, so office and industrial users should look elsewhere, and like most of this category, you'll need to ask for pricing.

What's good

  • Designed by retail analysts for real-world decisions
  • Trusted by major brands
  • Void analysis and territory planning are highly valuable
  • Easy-to-use interface for complex analytics

What's not

  • Pricing not publicly available
  • Primarily retail-focused
  • Training needed to get the most out of it

Pricing: SiteSeer Professional, annual subscription. Contact for pricing.

4. TestFit

Best for: Developers testing what a site can actually hold

Full Profile →

TestFit answers the developer's version of the site selection question: can I make a deal work on this parcel? Map the site, configure roads, and it generates 3D building massing with real-time financial analysis and automatic takeoffs for parking, infrastructure, and earthwork — in hours instead of the days a manual test fit takes. It claims 30x faster feasibility modeling, exports to Revit, AutoCAD, and SketchUp, and has 6,200+ users including major developers and architecture firms.

It's a feasibility tool, not a design tool — you'll still hand off to architects — and pricing requires a sales conversation.

What's good

  • 30x faster feasibility vs. traditional methods
  • 2-3x more design iterations on the same budget
  • Automatic cost takeoffs reduce estimation errors
  • Direct export to Revit, AutoCAD, SketchUp

What's not

  • Pricing not publicly listed
  • Feasibility stage only, not full architectural design
  • Learning curve on the AI optimization features

Pricing: Standard and Enterprise tiers, annual. Contact for pricing.

5. Zoneomics

Best for: Zoning due diligence before you fall in love with a site

Full Profile →

Zoneomics digitizes zoning and land use data from thousands of cities into the largest nationwide zoning dataset available, then layers AI analysis on top so you don't have to decode municipal code yourself. Users include Redfin, Cushman & Wakefield, and Moody's. The pricing model is refreshingly concrete: free basic zoning search, reports starting at $25, and API access on quote for bulk needs.

Coverage is the caveat — not every city is fully digitized, so check your target markets before relying on it, and budget more for certified zoning letters.

What's good

  • Largest nationwide zoning data coverage
  • Used by Redfin, Cushman & Wakefield, Moody's
  • AI analysis simplifies complex zoning codes
  • Weekly data updates; flexible API

What's not

  • Coverage varies by city — not all areas digitized
  • Certified zoning letters can get expensive
  • Free search is limited without buying reports

Pricing: Free zoning search. Reports from $25. API: contact for pricing.

6. LandVision (LightBox Vision)

Best for: Parcel research and ownership mapping

Full Profile →

LandVision — now rebranded as LightBox Vision — is the map-first workhorse for parcel research: nationwide parcel boundaries, building footprints, ownership data, and zoning in one interactive interface. The LightBox ID system links datasets across the LightBox ecosystem, and an ML-powered geocoder handles the address-matching grunt work. You can even order zoning reports without leaving the platform.

It's enterprise-oriented with a complex feature set, pricing is quote-only, and the rebrand from LandVision to LightBox Vision causes genuine confusion when you're researching it. Same product.

What's good

  • Nationwide coverage with comprehensive property data
  • LightBox ID links properties across datasets
  • AI-powered geocoder for precise matching
  • Zoning data and report ordering in-platform

What's not

  • Pricing not publicly available
  • Rebrand causes name confusion
  • Enterprise-oriented, complex feature set

Pricing: Annual subscription. Contact for pricing.

7. MapZot.AI

Best for: Chain-tracking and AI-driven sales forecasting

Full Profile →

MapZot.AI is the newer entrant here: a data and AI platform for site selection, audience research, and sales forecasting that lets multi-unit brands benchmark against local and national chains with property-level precision (92%+ claimed accuracy). It serves coffee chains, franchise developers, and CRE teams that want competitor movement and forecasting in one place.

It's young, which cuts both ways — modern AI tooling, but less track record than SiteZeus or Placer.ai, and quote-only pricing. Worth a demo if you're a multi-unit brand comparing against the incumbents.

What's good

  • 92%+ claimed accuracy with advanced data science
  • Property-level precision
  • Covers multiple industries
  • AI forecasting reduces expansion risk

What's not

  • Pricing not publicly available
  • Newer platform with less track record
  • Best suited to multi-unit brands

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact sales.

Comparison Summary

Product Core Question It Answers Pricing Free Tier
Placer.ai Who visits this location? Free tier; paid on quote Yes
SiteZeus Where should our chain expand? Quote-based No
SiteSeer Will retail work in this trade area? Quote-based No
TestFit What can I build here, and does it pencil? Quote-based No
Zoneomics What does zoning allow? Reports from $25 Yes (search)
LandVision Who owns this parcel, and what is it? Quote-based No
MapZot.AI How do we compare to competing chains? Quote-based No

Notice that these barely compete with each other. A retail chain might run Placer.ai for traffic, SiteSeer or SiteZeus for forecasting, and Zoneomics for due diligence. A developer might use LandVision to find the parcel, Zoneomics to check what's allowed, and TestFit to see if it pencils. The real decision is which questions you need answered, not which vendor wins.

The bottom line

Start free: Placer.ai's dashboard and Zoneomics' search cost nothing and will tell you quickly whether location data changes your decisions. If you're a multi-unit brand, the SiteZeus / SiteSeer / MapZot.AI demos are worth your time — bring your own store data and make each one forecast sites you already operate. Developers should look at TestFit and LandVision instead; they're solving a different problem than the retail tools, and no retail analytics platform will tell you whether the parcel pencils.
