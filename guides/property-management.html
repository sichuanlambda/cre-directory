BUYER'S GUIDE

Best Property Management Software for Commercial Real Estate (2026)

Updated October 2026 · 12 min read

TL;DR

Property management software in CRE ranges from $1/unit/month to enterprise pricing that requires a phone call. The right pick depends on your portfolio size and whether you need a single platform or best-of-breed tools. Here's how the main options compare.

There are a lot of property management platforms out there. Most of them were built for residential landlords and bolted on "commercial" as an afterthought. A few were built for commercial from day one. That difference matters more than any feature comparison.

This guide covers the 7 platforms CRE operators would realistically shortlist. Not the ones with the biggest marketing budgets. The ones that keep coming up in public sources as what operators run their portfolios on.

A couple things to know before the list:

  • Pricing in CRE software is weirdly opaque. Half these companies won't tell you what they charge until you sit through a demo. We've included what we could find, but take "contact for pricing" to mean "it's not cheap."
  • "Best" depends on your portfolio. Managing 50 units of multifamily is a different problem than managing 2 million square feet of office space. We've tried to be clear about who each tool is for.

The 7 Best Property Management Platforms for CRE

1. Yardi

Best for: Enterprise and mixed-use portfolios

Full Profile →

Yardi is the 800-pound gorilla. If you manage commercial, multifamily, affordable housing, senior living, or any combination, Yardi probably has a product for it. Their Voyager platform handles the heavy stuff. Yardi Breeze is their attempt at making it accessible for smaller operators, starting at $1/unit/month.

The catch: Yardi's full platform is complex. Implementation takes months, not days. And the pricing for Voyager-level products isn't something you'll find on a website. You're calling a sales rep and sitting through demos.

What's good

  • Covers every property type and asset class
  • Single-stack platform (accounting, leasing, maintenance, all in one)
  • Yardi Breeze is genuinely affordable for small operators
  • Global reach with clients in 80+ countries

What's not

  • Full platform is expensive and complex to implement
  • UI feels dated compared to newer competitors
  • Steep learning curve for non-technical users
  • Customization often requires professional services

Pricing: Yardi Breeze from $1/unit/month. Breeze Premier from $2/unit/month. Voyager and enterprise products: contact sales.

2. RealPage

Best for: Large multifamily operators

Full Profile →

RealPage focuses on multifamily and does it well. Their revenue management (AI-driven pricing optimization) is probably their strongest feature. If you operate thousands of apartment units and want to squeeze every dollar out of rental pricing, RealPage's algorithms are hard to beat.

They've been in the news for their pricing algorithm controversy (the DOJ got involved), which is worth knowing about. The product itself is powerful but firmly in the enterprise camp. Small operators shouldn't bother looking here.

What's good

  • AI revenue management is best-in-class for multifamily
  • Strong analytics and market intelligence
  • Deep integration across leasing, payments, and maintenance

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing (no published rates)
  • Pricing algorithm has drawn regulatory scrutiny
  • Not built for commercial/office/industrial

Pricing: Contact for pricing. Enterprise-tier only.

3. AppFolio

Best for: Mid-market and growing portfolios

Full Profile →

AppFolio hits a sweet spot that Yardi and RealPage miss: modern UI, reasonable pricing, and enough features for serious operators without the 6-month implementation cycle. They've been pushing hard on AI features recently, and their mobile app is one of the better ones in the category.

The minimum portfolio size (50 units) means solo landlords are out. But if you're a property management company growing past the spreadsheet stage, AppFolio is usually on the shortlist.

What's good

  • Clean, modern interface that staff actually like using
  • Strong mobile app for on-the-go management
  • Built-in marketing and leasing tools
  • AI maintenance coordination is genuinely useful

What's not

  • 50-unit minimum cuts out smaller operators
  • Per-unit pricing adds up fast at scale
  • Commercial property features lag behind residential

Pricing: Core from $1.49/unit/month (50 unit minimum = ~$75/month floor). Plus tier available with more features.

4. Entrata

Best for: Multifamily with strong leasing needs

Full Profile →

Entrata sells one operating system instead of a stack of point tools. Accounting, leasing, marketing, resident services, maintenance and utility management all sit behind a single login. The company reports 32,000+ properties and 3.2M+ unit spaces across 700+ property management companies, and its AI layer (ELI) handles resident communications, review responses and workflow automation.

The catch for CRE operators: Entrata is a multifamily platform first. It lists commercial among the sectors it serves, alongside student, affordable, military and manufactured housing, but the commercial side is less mature. Pricing is by quote, so you won't know the number until you talk to sales.

What's good

  • Single-login platform with no third-party dependencies for core functions
  • Runs at very large scale (32,000+ properties)
  • AI (ELI) built in across communications and workflows
  • Covers multifamily, student, affordable, military and commercial

What's not

  • No published pricing
  • Complex platform with a steep learning curve
  • Commercial features are less mature than multifamily
  • Closed ecosystem with fewer third-party integrations

Pricing: By quote. No published rates and no free trial.

5. Buildium

Best for: Small-mid portfolios (50-500 units)

Full Profile →

Buildium is the rare platform in this category that puts its prices on the website. It covers the full cycle: leasing, tenant screening, rent collection, maintenance, accounting and owner reporting. It also handles community associations, student housing and affordable housing, and its Lumina AI adds automated communications and bill scanning.

It was built for residential managers, and that shows in what's missing. Commercial property management features are limited, so an office or retail portfolio will outgrow it quickly. The best features also sit at the top of the price list: the open API is only on the Premium plan.

What's good

  • Published pricing on every plan
  • Deep residential feature set, from leasing to owner reporting
  • Lumina AI for bill scanning and communications
  • Free trial before you commit

What's not

  • Limited commercial property management features
  • Cost climbs significantly with portfolio size
  • Open API only on the Premium plan

Pricing: Essential from $62/month. Growth from $192/month. Premium from $400/month. Free trial available.

6. TenantCloud

Best for: Small landlords / budget-conscious

Full Profile →

TenantCloud is the low-cost entry on this list. For a small monthly fee you get online rent collection, tenant screening, listing syndication to Apartments.com, Realtor.com and Rentler, accounting with QuickBooks integration, maintenance requests and e-signatures. Landlords, tenants, owners and service providers each get their own mobile app.

It is a residential rental tool. Its listed property types are single-family, multifamily and HOA communities, with no commercial types among them. Plans are capped by lease count, so the price steps up as the portfolio grows, and the lease builder and inspections only arrive on the higher tiers.

What's good

  • Low starting price ($15/month billed annually)
  • Separate mobile apps for landlords, tenants, owners and service providers
  • Tenant screening and listing syndication from the Starter plan
  • Integrates with QuickBooks and Google Calendar

What's not

  • Starter plan is limited to 10 leases
  • Lease builder and inspections only on higher tiers
  • Residential focus, with no commercial property types listed

Pricing: Starter from $15/month billed annually ($18 monthly) for up to 10 leases. Growth $29/month, Pro $50/month, Business from $100/month. Free trial available.

7. DoorLoop

Best for: Ease of use / quick setup

Full Profile →

DoorLoop, founded in 2019, pitches itself on speed: data migration in days, not months. The Starter plan covers online rent collection, a tenant portal, maintenance tracking, tenant screening through TransUnion, lease management with eSignatures, and accounting and reporting.

It has more for commercial owners than most small-portfolio tools. It supports mixed-use commercial properties, and CAM charges are included from the Pro plan up. That is also where the AI Assistant, workflows and QuickBooks sync live, so the Starter price is not the real price for most commercial users.

What's good

  • Quick setup, with data migration in days
  • CAM charges and budgeting on the Pro plan
  • Open API and Zapier integrations
  • Full functionality on mobile

What's not

  • $69/month starting price is high for very small landlords
  • AI and workflows require Pro or higher
  • Per-unit pricing adds up on large portfolios

Pricing: Starter from $69/month billed annually ($99 monthly) for up to 10 units, then $3 per additional unit. Pro $149/month, Premium $209/month. Custom quote for 300+ units. Free trial available.

How We Compared These Tools

We didn't run a survey or score things on a 100-point rubric. Working from vendor documentation, published pricing and public sources, we looked at what CRE professionals actually care about when picking property management software:

  • What property types does it actually support? A lot of "commercial" PM software is really residential with a different label.
  • What does it cost, and how? Per-unit, flat fee, or enterprise pricing changes the math completely depending on your portfolio.
  • Can your team actually use it? The most powerful software doesn't help if your property managers can't figure it out.
  • What's the integration story? Property management doesn't exist in isolation. It needs to talk to your accounting, your leasing, your maintenance systems.
  • How long does implementation take? Some of these are up and running in a day. Others take months with professional services.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between residential and commercial property management software?

Residential PM software handles leases, rent collection, and maintenance for apartments and single-family homes. Commercial PM adds CAM reconciliation, NNN lease tracking, tenant improvement management, and often connects to investment/asset management tools. If you manage office, retail, or industrial space, you need software that understands commercial lease structures.

How much does CRE property management software cost?

It ranges wildly. TenantCloud starts at $15/month for small portfolios. Buildium and DoorLoop start around $60-70/month. AppFolio and Yardi Breeze charge per unit ($1-2/unit/month). Enterprise platforms like RealPage and Yardi Voyager don't publish pricing and typically require annual contracts.

Can I manage both residential and commercial properties on one platform?

Yardi is the strongest option for mixed portfolios. AppFolio and Buildium handle both but are stronger on the residential side. If commercial is your primary business, make sure to evaluate the commercial-specific features (lease abstraction, CAM, tenant billing) before committing.

Should I pick a single platform or use separate best-of-breed tools?

Single platform is simpler and cheaper for most operators. Best-of-breed makes sense if you have specific needs that no single platform handles well (like pairing a strong PM tool with a separate investment analysis platform). The integration overhead of running 4-5 separate tools is real, though.
