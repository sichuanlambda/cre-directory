BUYER'S GUIDE

Best Tenant Experience Platforms for CRE (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 10 min read

TL;DR

"Tenant experience" spans four different products: engagement platforms (HqO, VTS), building operations with a tenant layer (Building Engines, MRI Angus), access control (Brivo), and smart-building hardware (SmartRent). Every single one is quote-priced, and nearly all are enterprise-oriented. The apps that get used are the ones that gate something tenants need daily — door access, visitors, room booking.

Tenant experience became a budget line the moment hybrid work gave office tenants a genuine alternative to showing up. When the building has to compete with the kitchen table, the in-building experience stops being a nice-to-have. That's the honest pitch behind every product below.

The dirty secret of the category is adoption. A tenant app that only pushes event announcements gets deleted in a month. The platforms that work are the ones tied to things tenants must do — get through the turnstile, register a visitor, book a conference room, file a maintenance ticket. Keep that test in mind while reading vendor decks.

Our directory lists 10 products in this category. We cut the ones that aren't really software for CRE landlords (a delivery logistics service, a vehicle-sharing platform, and a digital health company all snuck into this category), plus one residential leasing tool, and kept 6.

Things to know before the list:

  • Nobody publishes pricing. All six are quote-based. Most sell per building or per portfolio, and several also involve hardware.
  • Know which layer you're buying. An engagement app, an operations platform, and an access control system are complementary, not interchangeable. Some buildings need all three; most need to start with one.
  • Commercial vs. multifamily matters. Most of these are office-first. SmartRent is the exception — it's multifamily-first, included here because the category covers both.

The 6 Best Tenant Experience Platforms

1. HqO

Best for: Landlords making tenant experience a portfolio strategy

Full Profile →

HqO calls itself a Real Estate Experience (REX) platform — effectively a CRM for buildings, unifying tenant engagement with data-driven intelligence so landlords can see how tenants actually use the portfolio and act on it. It scales from a single building to a global portfolio and is squarely aimed at CRE leaders who want experience to show up in financial performance, not just in an app store rating.

It's an enterprise product with enterprise implications: premium pricing, real training and change management, and more platform than a smaller landlord needs. It's also focused on experience rather than broader property management, so it sits alongside your PM system, not instead of it.

What's good

  • Purpose-built CRM approach to commercial real estate
  • Comprehensive tenant experience management
  • Strong AI-powered analytics and insights
  • Scales from one building to global portfolios

What's not

  • Experience-focused — not broader property management
  • Enterprise complexity for smaller landlords
  • Requires training and change management
  • Premium pricing

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact for custom pricing.

2. VTS

Best for: Large landlords who want tenant experience inside their leasing platform

Full Profile →

VTS comes at tenant experience from the asset management side. It's the platform the world's largest landlords already use for leasing and asset management — over 13 billion square feet managed — and it has expanded into a unified system covering leasing, marketing, property operations, and tenant experience. If you're already running your leasing pipeline on VTS, adding the experience layer means one platform instead of another point solution.

The catch is who it's for: enterprise landlords. Small brokerages and owners aren't the audience, pricing is opaque until you talk to sales, and teams coming from spreadsheets face a real learning curve. Multifamily features are newer than the office core.

What's good

  • Industry's largest CRE dataset (13B+ SF)
  • One platform across leasing, marketing, and tenant experience
  • AI features that solve real workflow problems
  • Proven at enterprise scale with major landlords

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing — out of reach for small firms
  • No pricing transparency before sales contact
  • Steep learning curve from simpler tools
  • Office-first; multifamily features are newer

Pricing: Quote-based, enterprise. Contact sales.

3. Building Engines

Best for: Property teams where operations drive the experience

Full Profile →

Building Engines, owned by JLL Technologies, starts from a blunt truth: the biggest driver of tenant satisfaction is whether the building actually works. It covers work orders, preventive maintenance, vendor management, inspections, certificate-of-insurance tracking, and tenant communication — the operational machinery behind a good experience, with tenant-facing tools layered on top.

It's enterprise-priced, the UI can feel dated next to newer rivals, and it's light on property accounting. And since it's a JLL company, firms working with competing brokerages may hesitate.

What's good

  • Purpose-built for commercial building operations
  • Strong risk management (COI, inspections, incidents)
  • Backed by JLL Technologies
  • Good tenant experience tools

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only
  • JLL ownership may not suit everyone
  • UI feels dated vs. newer competitors
  • Limited accounting features

Pricing: Quote-based, annual. Contact sales.

4. MRI Angus

Best for: Office buildings already in (or open to) the MRI ecosystem

Full Profile →

MRI Angus (formerly Angus Systems) is Building Engines' closest rival: service requests, maintenance management, visitor management and access, occupant communication, insurance document management, and shared resource booking in one platform. It won the 2025 IDC SaaS CSAT Award for Facilities Management, which says something real about how customers rate it, and it's mobile-friendly for both tenants and building staff.

It now lives inside MRI Software, so evaluate whether you're comfortable with that broader ecosystem. It's also office-focused and, as usual for this list, quote-priced.

What's good

  • IDC SaaS CSAT Award winner for FM (2025)
  • Comprehensive building operations in one platform
  • Strong visitor security and COI management
  • Mobile-friendly for tenants and staff

What's not

  • May require broader MRI commitment
  • Pricing not public
  • Less standalone than Building Engines
  • Primarily office-focused

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact sales.

5. Brivo

Best for: Access control as the foundation of the tenant experience

Full Profile →

Brivo is the largest cloud access control provider — 100M+ credentials and 24M+ users — and its Security Suite bundles access control, video surveillance, visitor management, identity, and intrusion detection under one cloud platform. Why is a security company on a tenant experience list? Because mobile credentials on the front door are the single most reliable driver of daily app usage a building has, and Brivo's open API lets property management platforms build on top of it.

Budget for more than software: hardware, professional installation, and add-on subscriptions for some features. Small properties will find the pricing enterprise-shaped.

What's good

  • Largest cloud access control provider
  • Unified access, video, visitor, and intrusion platform
  • Mobile credentials and smart locks
  • Open API for PM platform integrations

What's not

  • Hardware costs on top of subscription
  • Requires professional installation
  • Enterprise pricing, hard for small properties
  • Some features need extra subscriptions

Pricing: Subscription plus hardware. Contact for pricing.

6. SmartRent

Best for: Multifamily operators adding smart-home experience

Full Profile →

SmartRent is the multifamily entry on this list: smart home hardware (locks, thermostats, sensors) tied to cloud-based enterprise software, serving 600+ clients across 1.3M rental homes with 3.5M connected devices. For residents, the experience is mobile access, climate control, and self-guided convenience; for operators, it's reduced costs and — per SmartRent — $30-60 of added value per unit per month.

If you run office or industrial, this isn't your tool. And like all hardware-heavy platforms, there's an upfront installation investment plus the ongoing reality of maintaining IoT devices at scale.

What's good

  • Proven scale: 600+ clients, 1.3M homes, 3.5M devices
  • Claimed $30-60 added value per unit per month
  • Integrates with existing PM systems
  • Wide range of compatible smart hardware

What's not

  • Multifamily-first, not commercial
  • Upfront hardware and installation costs
  • Staff training required
  • Ongoing IoT maintenance and support

Pricing: Quote-based. Contact for custom pricing.

Comparison Summary

Product Layer Property Focus Pricing
HqO Experience & engagement Office / mixed-use Quote-based
VTS Leasing + experience platform Office-first Quote-based
Building Engines Operations + tenant layer Commercial / office Quote-based
MRI Angus Operations + tenant layer Commercial / office Quote-based
Brivo Access control & security Commercial + multifamily Subscription + hardware
SmartRent Smart-home hardware + software Multifamily Quote-based + hardware

The real comparison is HqO vs. VTS at the engagement layer and Building Engines vs. MRI Angus at the operations layer. Brivo and SmartRent aren't alternatives to those — they're the physical infrastructure that makes an experience app worth opening.

The bottom line

Fix operations before you buy engagement. A tenant app on top of slow work orders and broken door access makes the problems more visible, not better — which argues for starting with Building Engines or MRI Angus (plus Brivo if access is the pain point) and adding HqO once the fundamentals hold. Enterprise landlords already on VTS should price its experience layer before adding another vendor. Multifamily operators: SmartRent is the one on this list actually built for you. In every case, ask vendors for references from buildings like yours — a Class A trophy tower proves nothing about a suburban office park.
