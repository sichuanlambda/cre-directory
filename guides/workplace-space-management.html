BUYER'S GUIDE

Best Workplace & Space Management Software (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 11 min read

TL;DR

This category is dominated by enterprise IWMS platforms — Archibus (Eptura), Planon, Accruent, IBM TRIRIGA, Tango, Nakisa — and every one of them is quote-priced with a serious implementation. The honest differences are geography, industry focus, and what else you need bundled (lease accounting, sustainability, retail analytics). If your problem is narrower — planning and designing space rather than administering it — Saltmine is the modern specialist worth a look. Small teams with simple needs should probably not buy an IWMS at all.

Hybrid work broke the old assumptions about office space, and workplace management software is where organizations go to figure out what they actually need. The category runs from full Integrated Workplace Management Systems (IWMS) — space, assets, maintenance, leases, sustainability in one platform — down to focused tools for planning and designing workplaces.

We looked at the 16 products in our directory's workplace and space management category and picked the 7 a corporate real estate or facilities team would realistically shortlist. Fair warning: this is the most enterprise-heavy category we cover. Six of the seven don't publish pricing, and most assume an implementation project, not a signup form.

Things to know before the list:

  • "IWMS" means big. These platforms are designed for organizations with dozens or hundreds of locations. If you have three offices and want desk booking, an IWMS is overkill — and the vendors' own positioning says so.
  • This category has been through a merger blender. Archibus is now part of Eptura. TRIRIGA is being folded into IBM Maximo. Buying here means buying the acquirer's roadmap, not just the product.
  • Lease accounting keeps showing up. Several of these platforms (Accruent, Nakisa, TRIRIGA) bundle ASC 842/IFRS 16 compliance. If you already own a lease accounting tool, don't pay for it twice.

The 7 Best Workplace & Space Management Platforms

1. Archibus (Eptura)

Best for: Enterprise IWMS with the longest track record

Full Profile →

Archibus has been doing facility and real estate management since 1982 and is now the anchor of Eptura's worktech ecosystem (the iOFFICE + SpaceIQ merger), which powers 50% of the Fortune 500. The platform covers space planning, asset management, preventive maintenance, visitor management, and employee experience, with Autodesk integration for space planning and FedRAMP authorization for government and security-sensitive deployments.

The usual enterprise caveats apply — quote-only pricing, and a platform that's genuinely too much for smaller organizations — plus the transitional confusion of the Eptura rebrand. But if you want the most proven IWMS on the market, this is the reference point everything else gets compared to.

What's good

  • Powers 50% of the Fortune 500
  • FedRAMP authorized for government clients
  • Full IWMS: space, assets, facilities, employees
  • Autodesk integration for space planning

What's not

  • No public pricing
  • Overkill for smaller organizations
  • Eptura rebrand causes confusion

Pricing: Quote-based; contact sales.

2. Planon

Best for: Smart-building and sustainability-led portfolios, especially in EMEA

Full Profile →

Planon is the analyst darling of this category — recognized as a leader by Verdantix, Gartner, IDC, and Frost & Sullivan — with 40+ years behind its IWMS. It covers portfolio management, space and workplace management, facilities and maintenance, lease accounting, and field services, and it's notably strong on sustainability tracking and smart-building integration.

Its center of gravity is Europe (it's headquartered in the Netherlands), and its US presence is thinner than Archibus or Accruent. Like its peers, it's enterprise-priced, and implementations typically require specialized consulting.

What's good

  • Leader per Verdantix, Gartner, IDC, Frost & Sullivan
  • Strong sustainability and smart-building features
  • True full-scope IWMS
  • 40+ years of global enterprise experience

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only
  • Complex, consultant-led implementations
  • Primarily EMEA-focused; smaller US footprint

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact for pricing.

3. Accruent

Best for: Multi-industry portfolios needing facilities + lease compliance

Full Profile →

Accruent, a Fortive subsidiary based in Austin, spans space management, lease administration and accounting (ASC 842 and IFRS 16), capital planning, maintenance management, and IoT monitoring. Its distinguishing trait is industry breadth: healthcare, retail, education, and government alongside commercial real estate, which matters if your portfolio doesn't look like a standard office footprint.

That breadth came partly from acquisitions, and it shows — the product portfolio sprawls, and some module UIs feel dated. Pricing and implementation are standard enterprise fare.

What's good

  • IWMS covering real estate, facilities, and assets
  • Strong ASC 842 / IFRS 16 lease compliance
  • Serves healthcare, retail, education, government
  • Fortive backing for financial stability

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing out of reach for small orgs
  • Complex implementation
  • Acquisition-driven product sprawl; dated UI in places

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact for pricing.

4. Tango

Best for: Retail and corporate portfolios, from site selection onward

Full Profile →

Tango covers the whole property lifecycle — site selection, lease management, space utilization, projects and maintenance, energy and sustainability — on one connected platform. Its retail heritage shows in features like store performance prediction, which nothing else on this list offers, and its pitch centers on reducing an organization's second-largest expense: real estate.

It's aimed at large corporate and retail portfolios; if you're a brokerage or investment shop, this isn't your tool. Enterprise pricing and multi-location rollout complexity are the costs of entry.

What's good

  • Full lifecycle: site selection to sustainability
  • Strong retail features incl. store performance prediction
  • Connected platform reduces RE/FM data silos
  • Serious sustainability and energy tooling

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only
  • Built for large corporate/retail portfolios
  • Little investment/brokerage functionality
  • Implementation complexity at many locations

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact for pricing.

5. Nakisa

Best for: Tying real estate into enterprise HR and finance

Full Profile →

Nakisa comes at IWMS from an unusual angle: it also builds enterprise HR and finance software, so its real estate suite — capital projects, portfolio management, facility management, and lease accounting across ASC 842, IFRS 16, and GASB 87 — plugs into workforce planning and financial forecasting for enterprise-wide visibility. AI-driven decision intelligence runs across the modules.

It has less CRE-specific market recognition than Planon or Accruent, and the breadth of its portfolio means some real-estate corners get less depth than specialist tools. Enterprise-only pricing, as you'd expect.

What's good

  • IWMS + lease accounting for all major standards
  • Integrates RE with HR and finance systems
  • AI-powered decision intelligence
  • Covers capital projects through facility management

What's not

  • Enterprise pricing only
  • Less CRE market recognition than rivals
  • Breadth over depth in some CRE areas
  • Complex full-IWMS deployment

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact for pricing.

6. IBM TRIRIGA

Best for: Massive corporate occupiers already in the IBM ecosystem

Full Profile →

TRIRIGA — now packaged as IBM Maximo Real Estate and Facilities within the Maximo Application Suite — is the Fortune 500 IWMS: real estate portfolio management, lease accounting compliance, space management, facilities maintenance, capital projects, and sustainability, at whatever scale you need. Folding into Maximo also connects it to IBM's broader asset management stack.

The trade-offs are the bluntest on this list: it's very expensive, implementations are complex enough to often require IBM consultants, and the Maximo transition creates roadmap uncertainty. Our directory's own take is that it's overkill for most CRE firms — it's built for massive corporate occupiers, and that's who should buy it.

What's good

  • Enterprise-grade IWMS for Fortune 500 scale
  • RE, facilities, space, and sustainability in one suite
  • Maximo integration broadens asset management
  • Strong lease accounting compliance

What's not

  • Very expensive — IBM enterprise pricing
  • Implementations often need IBM consultants
  • Maximo transition creates uncertainty
  • Overkill for most CRE firms

Pricing: Enterprise, annual billing; contact IBM for pricing.

7. Saltmine

Best for: Workplace planning and design, not administration

Full Profile →

Saltmine is the one modern specialist on this list. Built by architects and real estate professionals (founded 2018), it handles space programming, test fitting, visualization, and project delivery for corporate real estate teams. Its claimed results are specific: 70-90% faster space programming, 5-10% fewer change orders, and 3-5% smarter space use — which translates directly into rent and project savings. Unlimited users, no specialized skills required, SOC 2 compliant.

It's not an IWMS — no maintenance, no lease administration — and it still carries quote-based pricing and a 4-8 week implementation. But if your actual problem is "what should our workplace be," rather than "how do we administer what we have," Saltmine attacks it more directly than any platform above.

What's good

  • 70-90% faster space programming
  • 5-10% reduction in change orders
  • Unlimited users, no special skills needed
  • SOC 2 compliant, enterprise-grade security

What's not

  • Pricing not publicly available
  • 4-8 week implementation timeline
  • Results depend on quality data inputs

Pricing: Quote-based; contact sales.

Yes, every row says "quote-based." That's the category. The practical comparison isn't price — you won't know it until the second sales call anyway — it's fit: which platform's center of gravity (government, sustainability, retail, HR/finance, sheer scale, or design) matches your portfolio, and how much implementation your team can survive.

The Bottom Line

If you're a large occupier choosing a system of record, start your shortlist with Archibus for pedigree and government-grade compliance, Planon if sustainability and smart buildings lead your requirements (especially in Europe), and Accruent if your portfolio spans industries. Tango is the pick when retail is your business, Nakisa when real estate needs to talk to HR and finance, and TRIRIGA when you're already an IBM shop at IBM scale. If your real problem is designing and right-sizing the workplace rather than administering it, evaluate Saltmine before committing to any IWMS — it's a faster path to the decisions hybrid work actually forced. And if you're small: book demos reluctantly. Every vendor here will happily sell you more platform than you need.
